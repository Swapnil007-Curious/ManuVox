\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Isolated sign language recognition.}
Isolated sign recognition assigns one gloss to a clip that contains a single sign. Video
benchmarks such as WLASL \citep{li2020wlasl} and ASL Citizen \citep{desai2023aslcitizen} made the
task measurable at the scale of a few thousand signs, and INCLUDE \citep{sridhar2020include} did so
for Indian Sign Language. ASL Citizen evaluates only on signers unseen in training and targets
webcam-based dictionary lookup, the setting closest to ours; we follow its signer-disjoint
protocol, test on its official test split, and train a second model on part of its training split.

\paragraph{Landmark models and the Google ISLR data.}
Pose-based models classify body and hand keypoints instead of pixels, which removes appearance and
makes on-device inference cheap: SPOTER uses a small transformer \citep{bohacek2022spoter}, and
OpenHands releases such models for several sign languages \citep{selvaraj2022openhands}.
\citet{moryossef2021pose} show what the skeletal representation loses with two pose estimators:
a model is only as good as its serving-time landmarks. Segmenting signs is a task of its own
\citep{renz2021segmentation}; our segmenter is a simple rule on hand motion. The Google ISLR
competition \citep{kaggle2023islr} released MediaPipe Holistic landmarks rather than video,
collected with the partners behind the PopSign game \citep{starner2023popsign}. We found no
peer-reviewed description of the top entries, and the leaderboard scored a hidden test set, so we
compare only against baselines trained under our own protocol. In the browser we use MediaPipe's
Tasks hand \citep{zhang2020mediapipehands} and pose \citep{bazarevsky2020blazepose} landmarkers.

\paragraph{Train--serve skew, shift and calibration.}
Faults accumulate unnoticed in the code around a deployed model \citep{sculley2015debt}, and
production checklists test that training and serving compute the same features
\citep{breck2017mltestscore}; live input also differs in distribution from the dataset, and our
live gap mixes both. Temperature scaling corrects miscalibration, which the expected calibration
error summarizes \citep{guo2017calibration}; selective prediction abstains below a threshold
\citep{geifman2017selective}, as our ``not sure'' does. Calibration degrades under dataset shift
\citep{ovadia2019shift}, as we observe across datasets and live.

\paragraph{Personalization from a few examples.}
Prototypical networks classify a query by its distance to class examples in an embedding space
\citep{snell2017prototypical}. Teach Dex, our personalization feature, borrows the idea without
retraining: for the signs a user has taught, it adds the cosine similarity between a segment's
embedding and the user's examples to the frozen model's calibrated logits, with a weight fitted on
validation signers rather than on the user's clips.
