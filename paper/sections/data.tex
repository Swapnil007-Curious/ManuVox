\section{Data and Splits}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We use the Google ISLR data \citep{kaggle2023islr} (licence CC BY 4.0): \V{data.total} sequences
of MediaPipe Holistic landmarks for \V{data.signs} isolated ASL signs, recorded by
\V{data.signers} signers. Each frame holds \V{data.lmtotal} landmarks (\V{data.lmface} face,
\V{data.lmpose} pose, \V{data.lmhand} per hand) with x and y normalized by the image width and
height. We drop sequences with fewer than \V{data.minhand} frames in which a hand is detected
(\V{data.excluded} sequences), leaving \V{data.kept}; each sign keeps between \V{data.signmin} and
\V{data.signmax} sequences.

\paragraph{Signer-independent split.}
Our split assigns signers whole, by a seeded random permutation, to training
(\V{data.trainsigners} signers, \V{data.trainseq} sequences), validation (\V{data.valsigners},
\V{data.valseq}) and test (\V{data.testsigners}, \V{data.testseq}); it is not an official split.
Checkpoint selection, early stopping, temperature fitting and the abstention threshold use
validation only, and a single script reads the test split and logs every run
(Table~\ref{tab:testaccess}). The test split was not untouched during development: the v2
features, recipe and architecture were designed after the v1 models had been evaluated on it, and
the model to ship was chosen by test accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:models}). The paired comparisons
and test calibration come from logits saved after those choices were final.

\paragraph{A second dataset: ASL Citizen.}
ASL Citizen \citep{desai2023aslcitizen} holds \V{cit.all.videos} webcam videos of
\V{cit.all.glosses} glosses by \V{cit.all.signers} signers with official signer-disjoint splits
(\V{cit.all.trainsigners}, \V{cit.all.valsigners} and \V{cit.all.testsigners} signers); its
licence allows non-commercial research and forbids redistribution. Its glosses are ASL-LEX entry
names, so \V{cit.map.mapped} of our \V{cit.map.vocabulary} labels map to them: \V{cit.map.exact} by
name, \V{cit.map.variant} to a numbered variant and \V{cit.map.synonym} to a synonym. The variant
and synonym choices were made with AI assistance from ASL-LEX's English translations and are not
confirmed by a signer; they are part of the method and a possible source of error. We extract
Holistic landmarks from the mapped videos with the training settings and preprocess them as
below. The official test split, \V{cit.videos} videos of \V{cit.signs} signs by \V{cit.signers}
signers (\V{cit.excluded} more had too few hand frames), is read by a second logged script once
per model: for v2 it is a cross-dataset test. The training and validation videos
(\V{v3.cit.train} and \V{v3.cit.val}) serve only to train v3. No ASL Citizen video, landmark or
per-video prediction is released, and v3 is not deployed.

\paragraph{Landmarks and features.}
One file, \cfg{features\_spec.json}, read by both the Python code and the browser, specifies the
preprocessing. Face and lip points and z are not used, which keeps the trackers' different depth
scales out of the model. Frames without a hand are removed;
coordinates are centered on the median shoulder midpoint and scaled by the median shoulder
distance; a sequence whose left hand is present in more frames than its right is mirrored
(x\,$\to$\,1$-$x with the hand blocks and left/right pose points swapped; Appendix~\ref{app:mirror});
and each sequence is linearly resampled to \V{data.frames} frames. The \emph{v1} input keeps both
hands and \V{data.v1pose} pose points (nose, shoulders, elbows, wrists), \V{data.v1points}
landmarks in all. The \emph{v2} input keeps \V{data.v2pose} pose points (adding eyes, ears and
mouth corners), \V{data.v2points} landmarks, and computes \V{data.v2feat} features inside the model
graph: coordinates, velocity and acceleration, the \V{data.pairdist} pairwise distances of the
dominant hand, and \V{data.facedist} distances from its wrist and fingertips to the nose, mouth and
shoulders, all standardized with training statistics.
