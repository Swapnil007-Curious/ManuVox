\section{Models and Training}
\label{sec:models}

\paragraph{Architectures.}
All models take a sequence of \V{data.frames} frames and output \V{data.signs} logits. The
\emph{GRU} projects each frame and runs a two-layer bidirectional GRU \citep{cho2014gru}; the
\emph{CNN} is a stack of residual depthwise-separable temporal convolutions
\citep{howard2017mobilenets,bai2018tcn}; the \emph{Transformer} is a four-layer encoder
\citep{vaswani2017attention}. The \emph{hybrid} has three stages, each of two depthwise-convolution
blocks followed by one transformer block, then global average pooling over time and a linear head,
with head dropout switched on only in the second half of training. It is close to the Conformer
\citep{gulati2020conformer} and resembles convolution--transformer models that circulated in the
competition, none of which has an archival description. Velocity, the v2 distance features and
standardization are computed inside the network, so the exported ONNX file takes raw normalized
landmarks.

\paragraph{Training.}
The \emph{v1 recipe} trains for up to \V{hp.v1.epochs} epochs with AdamW, cosine decay after a
short warm-up, label smoothing and geometric and temporal augmentation, with early stopping on
validation top-1. The \emph{v2 recipe} is a bundle of changes: up to \V{hp.v2.epochs} epochs, a
lower learning rate, an exponential moving average of the weights (decay \V{hp.v2.ema}) used for
validation and the saved checkpoint \citep{polyak1992averaging}, and frame dropout, time masking,
landmark masking and hand rotation. Every signer-independent configuration is trained with
\V{hp.seeds} seeds (Table~\ref{tab:hparams}). Training ran on a Kaggle \V{m.gpu} GPU with mixed
precision; one hybrid run took about \V{m.d.trainmin} minutes. The v2 grid is a cumulative
ablation from the v1 CNN: v2 features, then the v2 recipe, then the hybrid architecture, each
step conditional on the previous ones.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:models} gives the results. Signer independence costs a lot: trained on a
signer-dependent split, the same CNN reaches \V{m.dep.cnn.top1}\% test top-1, against
\V{m.cnn.top1}\% when the test signers are unseen. Among the v1 models, the transformer's
difference from the CNN is \V{cmp.tf.sig} and the GRU is \V{cmp.gru.diff} points lower
(Table~\ref{tab:comparisons}). In the cumulative v2 ablation, the v2 features did not help
measurably with the CNN and the v1 recipe (\V{cmp.feat.diff} points); the v2 recipe added a small
\V{cmp.recipe.diff} points and the hybrid \V{cmp.arch.diff} points. The hybrid step also
multiplies the parameter count by \V{m.paramratio} and the training time by \V{m.timeratio}, so we
cannot attribute its gain to the architecture alone. These tests treat the \V{cmp.n} test
sequences as independent and average over seeds, so their intervals exclude seed and signer
variation. Per signer, the hybrid's top-1 ranges from \V{sig.d.min}\% to \V{sig.d.max}\%
(Table~\ref{tab:persigner}); it is better than the CNN for all four test signers, but with four
signers that consistency is weak evidence about new ones, and the recipe's small gain is within
signer-to-signer variation.

\paragraph{Choosing the model to ship.}
The hybrid reaches \V{m.d.top1}\% top-1 and \V{m.d.top3}\% top-3; averaging the three seeds'
logits gives \V{m.d.ens.top1}\% at \V{m.d.ens.mb}\,MB in fp16 and a p95 of \V{m.d.ens.p95} ms per
call. The shipping rule took the highest \emph{test} top-1 with a p95 browser latency under
\V{budget.ms} ms and a file under ten megabytes (Figure~\ref{fig:acclat}). The latency budget keeps
classification well under a tenth of a second after the segmenter ends a sign; the size cap is a
repository convenience, not a browser limit, and it alone excludes the more accurate ensemble.
Because the rule used the test split, we note that validation top-1 selects the same model
(\V{m.d.val}\% against at most \V{m.c.val}\% for the others).

\paragraph{Calibration and abstention.}
The fitted temperature is below one (\V{cal.T}), so scaling sharpens the probabilities: the raw
model is underconfident, its accuracy above its confidence in every bin
(Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}, left), consistent with the label smoothing used in training. On the
test split, temperature scaling lowers the expected calibration error (\V{cal.bins} equal-width
bins) from \V{cal.test.ece0} \V{cal.test.ece0ci} to \V{cal.test.ece1} \V{cal.test.ece1ci} and the
negative log-likelihood from \V{cal.test.nll0} to \V{cal.test.nll1}; after scaling the upper bins
are slightly overconfident. The abstention threshold chosen on validation (\V{cal.thr}\%) accepts
\V{cal.test.cov}\% of test sequences at \V{cal.test.sel}\% selective accuracy, close to its
validation operating point (\V{cal.val.cov}\% at \V{cal.val.sel}\%), rejecting
\V{cal.test.rejwrong}\% of wrong and \V{cal.test.rejright}\% of correct predictions; within the
dataset, the rule transfers to unseen signers. Section~\ref{sec:liveresults} shows how it behaves
live.
